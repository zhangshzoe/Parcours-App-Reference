# Parcours course content

The catalog contains 2,500 short lessons across eight themes. French, English and German each have 100 lessons for A1, A2, A2+, B1, B2, C1 and C2. Japanese has 100 lessons for each of N4, N3, N2 and N1; N4 begins with kana, core sentence patterns and zero-foundation support. A2+ is an internal transition track between A2 and B1, not an official CEFR or exam level. These are practice units, not an assessment proving mastery of an entire level.

Every route follows the same progression: lessons 1–50 establish core vocabulary, patterns and practical situations; 51–80 require transfer into changed situations; 81–95 combine listening and reading evidence with speaking and writing; 96–100 are independent readiness checks aimed at the next route. The interface recommends progression only after all 100 lessons are complete and both scored understanding checks in each final lesson are correct. This signals course coverage and readiness, not guaranteed proficiency or formal certification; independent performance and, where needed, an accredited exam remain necessary.

The French, English, German and Japanese dialogues, Chinese explanations, exercises and sample responses are original teaching material. The literary excerpt in lesson-54 is also original. Scenarios and example messages are fictional exercises, not legal, commercial or factual claims about named organizations.

Difficulty framing references the Council of Europe's [CEFR levels](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/level-descriptions) and [qualitative aspects of spoken language use](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-3-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-qualitative-aspects-of-spoken-language-use). German route labels and skill focuses reference the [Goethe-Institut A1–C2 exam route](https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/prf.html); English route labels reference [Cambridge English Qualifications](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/) and their CEFR mapping. The material is original and is not an official CEFR, Goethe, Cambridge, DELF or DALF curriculum, exam or certification. Course levels and sample answers should receive review from qualified teachers of the relevant language before high-stakes educational use.

Japanese route labels reference the official [JLPT competence summaries](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html) and [test-section descriptions](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html). The zero-foundation N4 route introduces kana, basic particles, polite forms and everyday communication before progressing to N3 bridging skills, N2 broader real-life comprehension and N1 complex, abstract material. JLPT officially assesses language knowledge, reading and listening; the speaking and writing work in this app is additional original practice and does not reproduce official test items.

The English module uses a New Concept English-inspired progression without copying or reproducing its texts: A1 establishes sentence patterns and core everyday grammar; A2 expands daily tense and functional language; A2+ connects sentences into short narratives; B1 develops narrative and practical writing; B2 moves into close reading, paraphrase and argument; C1 develops extended analysis and register; C2 focuses on nuance, rhetoric and audience-sensitive reformulation. Cambridge and CEFR labels remain assessment references, not an official one-to-one mapping to the New Concept English books. Each English level rotates through at least six distinct knowledge strands across its 100-lesson route.

The expanded catalog uses a spiral curriculum: learners revisit practical situations with progressively more demanding language and reasoning. Every lesson has six bilingual dialogue turns, three vocabulary expressions, a comprehension question, a grammar or discourse question with explanations, a writing task and a reference response. A2–C2 and N4–N1 also have a reading interpretation prompt and manual writing reflection checks. Speech uses the learner's device's matching French, English, German or Japanese speech synthesis voice. The bilingual switch controls whether Chinese support is shown by default. Text-length and punctuation checks do not assess proficiency or replace feedback from a teacher.

Suggested word ranges and minimum completion lengths are defined centrally in `lib/levels.ts`, shared by the interface and server. Existing lesson IDs, vocabulary IDs and completion rules are retained to preserve saved learning records. The six-question diagnostic currently remains French-only, scoped to A2–B1, and cannot assign advanced proficiency.
